Pogo Pad Survey

Ten contacts on the underside of the module press against sprung pins in the keyboard. Their positions came from a task brief, not from calipers — and unlike every other dimension on this board, they cannot be derived from a datasheet. The keyboard decides them.

10 pads 2 blocks · J4, J11 all Ø2.20 pitch 2.500 layer B.Cu board 19.30 sq

Get the orientation right before touching a caliper. These pads are on the underside. The drawing below is a bottom view — the face you see when the module is turned over — so X is mirrored relative to the design file.

The table carries both: design X as stored in the board, and seen-from-below X, which is simply its negative. Mixing them up mirrors the whole block and it will still look plausible. This project has already lost time to a top-view/bottom-view mix-up once, on the MC3651 pinout.

Three things worth resolving while you have it open

J4 and J11 do not sit on the same Y grid

J4's rows are at −7.220 / −4.720 / −2.220. J11's are at −7.200 / −4.700. A consistent 0.020 offset between two blocks that meet the same rigid keyboard.

It is small enough to be a typo carried forward and small enough not to matter — but nobody knows which, and those are different situations. If the two blocks really are on one grid, one of these sets is wrong by 20 µm.

Five of J4's six contacts connect to nothing

Only J4.5 carries a net, and it is ground. The other five are copper pads wired to nowhere — presumably functions of the knob module this replaces.

Worth confirming the keyboard actually drives six pins there. If it does, and any of them carry a signal or a supply, five landing pads sitting unconnected is a wasted opportunity at best and a short waiting to happen at worst.

Pin tip diameter versus pad diameter

Ø2.20 is what the board draws. What matters mechanically is the pogo pin's tip and how far it can wander before it walks off the edge under sideways load.

If the tip is much smaller than 2.20 there is margin; if it is comparable, position error eats it fast. This is the number that decides how much the other measurements are allowed to be wrong.

If a number comes back different

  1. Tell me the measured values

    Even one that disagrees is worth acting on. Pitch and block spacing are the two that propagate furthest.

  2. The pads move, and the board is re-routed

    Pad positions are an input to placement, so the twenty-odd passives around them shift and every track is laid again. It is automated, but it is the whole routing step.

  3. Preflight WARN 14 clears

    It reads "J4/J11 pogo pads — fixed by the keyboard; measurement, not a datasheet." It is one of two things standing between this board and an order, and the only one I cannot resolve myself.
